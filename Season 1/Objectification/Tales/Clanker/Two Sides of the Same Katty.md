# **Two Sides of the Same Katty**

An alternate-universe encounter

The room was white.

White walls. White ceiling. Two chairs, a small table, and a vending machine that didn't appear to contain anything edible.

Regular Katty had spent the first fifteen minutes checking the doors, the windows, and the corners of the ceiling. He'd tested the door handle six times.

Locked.

Naturally.

He sat with his arms folded, thick sleeves covering his hands, his expression sour enough to curdle milk.

Then the door opened.

"HI\!"

Katty nearly jumped out of his chair.

A woman walked into the room as though she'd just arrived at a birthday party. She looked almost exactly like him, except for the obvious differences in presentation, posture, and expression. She wore a grin that seemed physically incapable of disappearing.

She pulled out the opposite chair and sat backward on it, resting her arms over the backrest.

"OH MY GOD. LOOK AT US\!"

Regular Katty stared at her.

She stared back.

He narrowed his eyes.

She narrowed hers in imitation.

He frowned.

She frowned harder.

"Stop that."

"Stop what?"

"That."

"This?"

She pointed at her face.

"THIS?"

"YES, THAT\!"

She burst out laughing.

Regular Katty dragged both hands down his face.

"Oh, this is going to be a fucking nightmare."

"You're so grumpy\!"

"You're so annoying."

"You're me\!"

"Unfortunately."

She leaned forward.

"Can I hug you?"

"No."

"Can I touch your hair?"

"No."

"Can I poke your cheek?"

"No."

"Can I—"

"Finish that sentence and I'm breaking your fingers."

She beamed.

"THAT'S SO YOU\!"

He looked at the ceiling.

Please let this be a hallucination.

Unfortunately, he knew it wasn't.

Because he knew everything about her.

Every detail. Every year. Every decision.

And she knew everything about him.

Including the things he'd rather forget.

## **1\. The Unfortunate Similarities**

Kinky Katty swung her legs.

"So\! We have the same birthday."

"June ninth. Yes. I know."

"Same parents\!"

"Regrettably."

"Same species\!"

"Obviously."

"Same childhood\!"

"Up until a point."

She tilted her head.

"Do you ever think about how weird that is? We started out in almost exactly the same place, and then we went in completely different directions."

Regular Katty went quiet.

"...Sometimes."

Her grin softened slightly.

"I mean, I was excited when Dad said I could work at Mom's place. You were scared."

"I wasn't scared."

She blinked.

"You were terrified."

"I was fourteen."

"So was I\!"

"That doesn't make it better\!"

He shifted in his seat, pulling his thick jacket closer around himself.

"I didn't even understand what they were asking me to do. They just told me we needed money. I thought I was getting some ordinary job."

Kinky Katty listened without interrupting.

For once.

"And when I found out what the job actually was, I wanted out. Immediately. I told them I didn't want to do it. They didn't care."

His jaw tightened.

"They got paid more because of me. That's what mattered to them."

Kinky Katty looked down at her hands.

"...They got paid more because of me, too."

"Yeah. I know."

"But I didn't want to leave."

"I know that, too."

She glanced up.

"Does that make you hate me?"

He answered immediately.

"No."

Her ears perked up.

"Really?"

"I don't have to like your decisions to recognize that you're a different version of me."

"Oh."

She considered this.

Then she smiled again.

"That's actually a pretty mature answer."

"Don't sound so surprised."

"I'm not\! I'm impressed\!"

"Stop being impressed."

"Never."

He sighed.

There was something deeply irritating about talking to someone who could predict nearly every response he made.

It was like arguing with a mirror that had been dropped on its head.

## **2\. The Question About the Body**

Kinky Katty leaned back in her chair.

"So, the top surgery."

Regular Katty's expression immediately changed.

"What about it?"

"You got it when you were sixteen."

"Yes."

"And you didn't get bottom surgery."

"Correct."

"And you changed your pronouns."

"Yes."

"And you wear all those clothes because you hate people looking at your body."

His eyes narrowed.

"Careful."

She held up both hands.

"Okay, okay\! I'm asking because I want to understand."

He watched her for a moment before answering.

"I wanted to be comfortable in my own body. I wanted to look in a mirror without feeling like I was looking at someone else. I wanted people to stop treating me like I was something they could buy."

His voice became quieter.

"Getting the surgery helped. Changing my pronouns helped. Leaving helped."

He paused.

"None of it made what happened disappear, though."

Kinky Katty's expression had lost its usual theatrical brightness.

"I don't understand that feeling," she admitted.

"I know."

"I like my body. I like showing it off. I like people looking at me."

"Good for you."

"And you hate it."

"I hate people thinking they have a right to it."

She stopped swinging her legs.

"Oh."

"Those aren't the same thing."

"No," she said slowly. "I guess they aren't."

He looked at her.

"Do you ever get tired of people thinking they're entitled to you?"

She opened her mouth.

Closed it.

Then she smiled.

"Sometimes."

"Sometimes?"

"Well, not usually."

"Katty."

"What?"

"You're allowed to say something bothers you."

She gave him an odd look.

"But I don't mind most of the time."

"Then you don't mind. That's your choice. I'm talking about when you do mind."

She looked away.

For several seconds, the only sound was the vending machine humming.

"...I don't know what I'd do if I didn't want someone to have me," she finally said.

Regular Katty didn't respond immediately.

That answer bothered him more than her jokes had.

Not because she enjoyed attention.

Because she seemed unable to imagine a situation in which her own wishes would be enough to say no.

He leaned back.

"Well, I do."

She looked at him.

"I'd tell them to fuck off."

She laughed.

"There you are\!"

"I'm serious."

"I know\!"

"And if they didn't listen, I'd leave. Or fight. Or freeze the fucking floor beneath their feet."

"That last one sounds fun."

"It's self-defense."

"It can be two things."

He reluctantly conceded that point with a grunt.

## **3\. The Ice Magic Incident**

"Speaking of ice magic," Kinky Katty said, "show me\!"

"No."

"Please?"

"No."

"Pretty please?"

"No."

"I'll tell you a secret."

"I know all your secrets."

"I'll tell you one you don't know."

He paused.

She grinned triumphantly.

"...Fine. What?"

"I once tried to eat a candle because I thought it was made of flavored wax."

Regular Katty stared.

"...What?"

"I was eleven\! I was curious\!"

"You ate a candle?"

"I tried to\!"

"Why?"

"BECAUSE I WAS CURIOUS\!"

He stared at her for another five seconds.

Then he laughed.

It was a short, abrupt sound, as though it had escaped him against his will.

Kinky Katty immediately pointed at him.

"HA\! YOU LAUGHED\!"

"I did not."

"You DID\!"

"That was a cough."

"A COUGH?"

"A very unfortunate cough."

"You sound like a dying goose when you cough?"

"Keep talking and you'll find out what frostbite feels like."

She laughed so hard that she nearly fell off her chair.

Regular Katty shook his head.

Despite himself, the corner of his mouth remained slightly raised.

Then she noticed his hands.

"Wait. You really did learn martial arts?"

"Yes."

"At twenty-six?"

"Yes."

"And you're a black belt?"

"Yes."

"Show me a move\!"

"No."

"WHY NOT?"

"Because I don't want to demonstrate a martial art to someone who thinks eating candles is a reasonable experiment."

"IT WAS ONE TIME\!"

"You tried to eat a candle."

"I DIDN'T ACTUALLY EAT IT\!"

"That is not the defense you think it is."

She crossed her arms.

"You're no fun."

"And you're a walking safety warning."

"Thank you\!"

"That wasn't a compliment."

"I accept it anyway\!"

## **4\. Happy**

The conversation eventually slowed.

Kinky Katty had been asking about his life since leaving home, and Regular Katty had answered most of her questions.

Most.

"And Happy?" she asked.

His expression changed.

She noticed immediately.

"...Oh."

He looked down at the table.

"He died when I was twenty-four."

"I know."

"Right."

She hesitated.

"What was he like?"

For a moment, Regular Katty didn't answer.

Then he began talking.

"He was an idiot."

Kinky Katty blinked.

"He'd bark at things that weren't there. He'd run around in circles for no reason. He once stole an entire loaf of bread off a counter and then looked personally offended when I tried to take it back."

She smiled.

"Sounds wonderful."

"He was."

His fingers curled against his sleeves.

"He followed me everywhere. Even when I told him not to. Especially when I told him not to."

He looked toward the blank wall.

"I found him when I was on the road. I didn't have much. Barely knew where I was going. But he kept following me, so eventually I just... took him with me."

Kinky Katty said nothing.

"He was there for eight years."

His voice grew rougher.

"Eight years. That's longer than I lived with any other friend."

She swallowed.

"I don't have a Happy."

"No."

"Would you have let me meet him?"

Regular Katty glanced at her.

"...Probably."

Her face brightened.

"Really?"

"Yeah. He liked people."

She paused.

"Even people like me?"

He frowned.

"What's that supposed to mean?"

"I don't know. I'm not exactly the most responsible person."

"Happy once tried to befriend a trash can."

"...Oh."

"He wasn't picky."

She smiled again, but it was smaller this time.

"I'm sorry he died."

Regular Katty nodded.

"Thanks."

She didn't make a joke.

She didn't try to cheer him up.

She simply sat there with him.

And, strangely enough, he appreciated that more than he would have admitted.

## **5\. The Children**

Eventually, Regular Katty asked the question he'd been avoiding.

"What about your kids?"

Kinky Katty's smile faded.

"Oh."

"How many?"

She counted silently for a moment.

"Several."

"That's not a number."

"I know."

"How many, Katty?"

She gave him a number.

He stared at her.

"...And you sent some to orphanages."

"Yes."

"And some were sold to the business."

Her eyes dropped.

"...Yes."

The room became very quiet.

Regular Katty's hands tightened into fists inside his sleeves.

"You sold your own children."

"I know."

"To the same kind of place that—"

"I KNOW\!"

Her voice cracked through the room.

She looked startled by her own outburst.

For a few seconds, neither of them spoke.

Then she took a breath.

"I'm not proud of that part."

"Then why did you do it?"

She stared at the floor.

"I didn't think about what would happen to them. Or maybe I did, and I just didn't want to think about it."

She rubbed her arm.

"I liked my life. I liked doing what I wanted. I liked not having to worry about anybody else's expectations."

She paused.

"And when the children came along, I didn't want to be a mother."

"Then you shouldn't have been one."

"I know."

"You had choices."

"I know\!"

"Those children didn't."

She flinched.

Regular Katty stopped talking.

He was angry. Very angry.

But he also knew that yelling at her wouldn't change what had happened to those children.

Finally, he spoke again.

"Do you ever wonder where they are?"

Kinky Katty's mouth tightened.

"...Sometimes."

"Do you want to find them?"

She took a long time to answer.

"I don't know if they'd want to see me."

"That's not what I asked."

She looked up.

He met her gaze.

"Do you want to find them?"

"...Yes."

The answer was barely audible.

He leaned back, exhausted.

"Then start there."

She looked at him as though he'd handed her something unfamiliar.

"What if they hate me?"

"They might."

"What if they never forgive me?"

"They might not."

"What if—"

"Katty."

She stopped.

"You can't undo what you did. But you can stop pretending it doesn't matter."

She looked away.

For once, she had nothing cheerful to say.

## **6\. Who Is Happier?**

After a long silence, Kinky Katty suddenly asked, "Are you happy?"

Regular Katty blinked.

"What?"

"Are you happy? Like, with your life."

He considered the question.

"I have a place to sleep. I can choose what I wear. Nobody gets to decide what happens to my body. I can walk away from people I don't like. I can defend myself."

He paused.

"I miss Happy."

Kinky Katty nodded.

"And sometimes I have bad days. Sometimes I get angry over stupid things. Sometimes I feel like I'm still fourteen and stuck in that place."

His jaw tightened.

"But it's my life now."

He looked at her.

"So, yeah. I think I'm happy."

She smiled.

"That's good."

"What about you?"

"Oh, I'm happy\!"

The answer came so quickly that he almost believed it.

Almost.

She continued.

"I have money. I'm famous. I get to try new things. I get attention whenever I want it. I have fun."

"That's a list of things you enjoy. I asked if you're happy."

She frowned.

"Is there a difference?"

"Sometimes."

She thought about that.

"I don't know."

For the first time since entering the room, Kinky Katty looked genuinely confused.

She leaned her chin on the back of the chair.

"I don't really care what happens to me. If something bad happens, it happens. There are more important people in the world than me."

Regular Katty's expression hardened.

"No."

She blinked.

"No?"

"You're not going to tell me your life doesn't matter."

"But—"

"No. I don't care how much you enjoy what you do. I don't care how much attention you get. I don't care how famous you are."

He leaned forward.

"Your life matters, too."

She stared at him.

"But I'm—"

"You're Katty."

She went silent.

"You're me, in another universe. I know what you've done. I know what you like. I know what you hate. And I know you think everybody else matters more than you do."

He took a breath.

"But I'm telling you that you're wrong."

Her eyes widened slightly.

He sat back.

"You don't have to live like me. You don't have to dress like me, think like me, or want the things I want. You can make your own choices."

He paused.

"But you don't have to throw yourself away to make those choices."

Kinky Katty looked at him for a long time.

Then she smiled faintly.

"...You're a lot nicer than you pretend to be."

Regular Katty immediately scowled.

"Take that back."

"No."

"Take it back."

"Never."

"I will freeze your chair to the floor."

"Then we'll both be stuck here\!"

"...You're an idiot."

"And you're my alternate self\!"

"Unfortunately."

She giggled.

He sighed.

But this time, he didn't look away.

## **7\. Before They Leave**

Eventually, the door unlocked.

Neither of them moved.

Kinky Katty looked at the open doorway, then back at him.

"Do you think we'll ever see each other again?"

"Hopefully not."

"Aw\!"

"I'm serious."

"Me too\!"

He stood up and adjusted his jacket.

She got to her feet as well.

For a moment, they stood facing each other.

Two versions of the same person.

One who had spent years trying to reclaim control over his own life.

One who had spent years treating herself as though her life mattered less than everyone else's.

They shared a childhood, a family, and a birthday.

But they were not the same person.

Kinky Katty tilted her head.

"Can I ask you one last question?"

"What?"

"If you could go back and change everything that happened to you, would you?"

Regular Katty froze.

He thought about being ten and coming home from school in tears.

He thought about being fourteen and learning what his parents had planned for him.

He thought about the lights, the noise, the hands he hadn't wanted touching him.

He thought about running away at sixteen.

About the surgery.

About the first time someone called him he without hesitation.

About Happy.

About the black belt he'd earned at twenty-six.

He thought about all the things he'd lost.

And all the things he'd built afterward.

"...I don't know," he admitted.

Kinky Katty nodded.

"That's fair."

"What about you?"

She looked surprised.

Then she thought about her childhood, her fame, her choices, and her children.

For once, she didn't answer immediately.

"...I don't know either."

He nodded.

"Yeah."

She took a step toward the door, then stopped.

"Hey, Regular Katty?"

"What?"

"I'm glad you got away."

He stared at her.

She gave him a small, sincere smile.

"Really."

His expression softened.

"...I'm glad you told me about your kids."

She blinked.

"Even though I did all those terrible things?"

"Especially because of that."

He opened the door wider.

"You know what you did. Now you get to decide what you do next."

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then she nodded.

"Okay."

She stepped through the doorway.

"GOODBYE, ME\!"

"Goodbye, you absolute disaster\!"

She laughed as the door closed behind her.

Regular Katty stood alone in the white room.

He looked at the empty chair.

Then at the door.

Then he muttered, "God, I'm annoying."

A pause.

"...Actually, she's annoying."

He turned to leave.

And, for the first time that day, he was smiling.

Because for all their differences, he'd met someone who understood his entire life without needing it explained.

And she had met someone who could look at her worst decisions without pretending they weren't terrible—and still believe she could choose differently.

Neither of them had become the other.

Neither of them needed to.

They were both Katty Garnit.

And that was complicated enough.
