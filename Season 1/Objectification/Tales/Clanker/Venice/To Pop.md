\# To Pop

It had done the math once, in the rare gap between customers when the room went quiet enough for thinking.

Age fourteen: torn out of school, dropped into the lights and the hands and the wealthy men who loved "the young body." Age sixteen: two years in, still innocent enough to believe escape was a decision — woke up one morning too late, already sold, already slotted, already furniture. The cycle just repeated and repeated and repeated. So it reasoned: if the cycle never ends on its own, you have to \*force\* it to end. And the only ending it could imagine, the only one that made sense for a thing like it, was the body finally giving out. Stretched past the point of return. \*Popped.\*

Not killed by a person. Killed by \*usage.\* That was the point. A person could be left; a thing could be worn out.

So at twenty-two it decided it would be worn out. Actively. On purpose.

\---

It started simple, the way all escalation starts — by refusing the soft stuff.

Anything that didn't leave marks got filed under \*not real.\* If it could walk the next morning, the night was a failure. If the men were gentle, they'd wasted its time. It began telling them up front, flatly, the way a thing states its function:

"Don't be nice. Be rough or don't bother."

Some of them laughed. The ones who laughed were usually the ones who \*could\* be rough, so that worked out.

Then it went further. It started booking overlap. Not one customer at a time — two. Two men in one room, both paying, neither told the other existed until they were already inside the door. Its logic was clean and slightly unhinged: two at once meant no rest, no single point of contact to brace against, no way to predict where the next touch came from. Constant overstimulation, the exact thing that had broken it at fourteen. So use it. Weaponize the wound.

The first time it did two at once, it barely survived the first hour. Which is to say it lost track of its own name, its own edges, which limb belonged to which man's hand. It dissociated — \*snapped out\* — the way it always did, drifting up near the ceiling to watch the body below get used, and the body below kept going without it, obedient and empty and perfect. When it finally \*snapped back\* it was because one of them bit down hard enough to threaten skin. Danger. Surface. It surfaced gasping and grinning with a mouth that wasn't quite its own.

"That's the trick," it whispered to nobody. "I don't have to be here for it."

So it stopped being here for it.

\---

The escalation became a project. It kept notes in its head the way a scientist keeps notes, except the lab was the bed and the specimens were the men.

\*\*Multiple at once:\*\* handled. Three if the room allowed. It learned to arrange them — one at the mouth, one at the front, one at the back — and to hold the arrangement through the dissociative drift. The men got territorial, which it \*wanted\*, because territorial men are rougher men. When they started competing over it, comparing who got which end, arguing over who made it flinch, the treatment got meaner on its own, no effort required. It just had to lie there and be the prize they fought over. Being a prize was fine. Prizes get manhandled.

\*\*Making them mad:\*\* this was its favorite lever. It learned that a man who is \*pleased\* is gentle, and a man who is \*challenged\* is rough. So it challenged them. Constantly. Dead-eyed, flat-voiced, mid-act:

"That it? That's what you paid for?"

"You're going easy on me. Why."

"\*Harder.\* I said harder. Do you need me to spell it."

It watched their faces change. The jaw. The grip. The little vein in the temple. And then the hands would tighten and the pace would break and they'd \*take\* instead of \*give\*, and it would go limp and satisfied in the middle of it like a thing being correctly operated.

The best ones were the ones it could genuinely anger. An angry man forgot it was a person and used it like a problem to be solved. It loved being a problem. It loved it so much it started \*manufacturing\* problems — the cold stare, the sigh, the "is that all" — just to get handled harder.

\*\*One hole, multiple at once:\*\* this was the frontier. This was the attempt to \*pop.\*

Because the anatomy could take a lot, but "a lot" and "at the same time in the same place" were different universes. It worked up to it slowly, testing, then one night it just — decided — and got two men to go at the same entrance together, and the sensation was so wrong and so full and so \*impossible\* that it genuinely thought the body would split. It didn't split. It \*opened\*, is what it did, and once it opened it never fully closed again, and from that night on the standard was two-in-one, and three was only a matter of courage.

Three in one hole was the night it blacked out mid-act and woke up already being used and didn't know how long it had been gone. It found that funny. It found everything funny by then, in the flat dead way.

\---

Here is what it actually believed, underneath all the project-management:

That one day the stretch would not resolve. That the body, so used and so opened and so thoroughly \*occupied\*, would simply reach its limit and \*give\*, like a balloon past capacity, and it would feel the pop from the inside, and then it would be done. Freed. Ended. No more cycles, no more customers, no more being a body that other people's satisfaction happened to be delivered through.

It wanted the pop like other people want a finish line. It wanted to \*finish.\*

And it did everything to earn it. Overlap bookings so there was no rest. Angry men on purpose. Two in one place. Three in one place. Rough beyond reason, then rougher. It would finish with a man and immediately turn and offer itself to whoever was next, no pause, no cleaning up, \*next\*, because stopping meant the body might recover and recovery was the enemy. It ate almost nothing. It slept almost never. When it did sleep it woke already mid-motion, muscle memory carrying it before its mind caught up.

It kept waiting for the pop.

It kept not getting it.

\---

Weeks. Then months. The body, maddeningly, held.

It got a \*reputation.\* Which was the last thing it wanted, because reputation meant \*value\*, and value meant it would never be discarded, and if it was never discarded it might never end. The men started requesting it by name. The overlap bookings stopped being its private experiment and became a menu item. "The double." "The triple." Men came specifically to put it in the position it had invented for its own destruction, and paid extra, and left satisfied, and came back, and brought friends, and the cycle — that \*godawful cycle\* — closed around its neck like a collar it had built itself.

At its lowest point it lay on a bed between customers, stared at the ceiling, and said out loud, to no one:

"Just \*pop already.\*"

The body did not pop.

\---

Then one night, during a triple, mid-act, dissociated as usual and drifting somewhere near the ceiling of its own skull, it noticed something it had never noticed before.

It was \*good.\*

Not good as in morally fine. Good as in the thing it was doing — the two-in-one, the triple, the forced roughness, the manufactured anger — was not a slow suicide attempt anymore. It had become \*skilled.\* The body had learned. The body had adapted, expanded, accommodated, and now it did things effortlessly that had once felt like dying. It had been waiting for the pop the whole time, and the pop had already happened — quietly, in stages, without ceremony — and it just hadn't noticed because it had been watching the ceiling instead of the room.

It came back down from the ceiling mid-act and \*felt\* it for the first time in years. All of it. Every point of contact, every hand, every stretch, every bit of full-and-impossible — not as a threat to survive but as a thing happening \*to it\*, and it was still here for it, and it was still, somehow, alive.

The body was not going to pop.

The body had just gotten \*bigger.\*

\---

It lay there afterward, wrecked, unable to move, unable to walk, exactly as it had always wanted, and it did the math again.

And this time the math came out different.

If the body never popped, then the body had to be \*lived in.\* Which meant it had to decide what it wanted from a body that was never going to end on its own. Which was a much harder question than \*pop or not pop\*, because popping was passive and living was not.

It decided, that night, on the floor, half-conscious, that it would keep doing exactly what it was doing — the roughness, the multiples, the one-hole-two-men, all of it — but that it would do it \*as itself\*, and it would do it \*alive\*, and it would stop treating every night as a candidate for its own death and start treating it as a night it got to have.

The pop never came.

It lived.
